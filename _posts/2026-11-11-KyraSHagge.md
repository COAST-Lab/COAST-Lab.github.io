How can human collaboration and cooperation improve environmental outcomes? What happens if we bring people with different organizational background in one room and let them solve environmental challenges such as flooding collectively? How can we use the resources within social networks to protect our environment?
Hi, I am  <strong> Kyra S. Hagge </strong> - the first ever postdoc with the COAST Lab (or so I have been told) and questions like these are on the forefront of my research. <br>

As a interdisciplinary social scientist with a background in Economics, Social Psychology, and Hydrogeology, my work integrates with the labs interest in outreach and participatory science. In my PhD dissertations, I have looked at how individual decisions on wastewater treatment impact nutrient pollution in the Tar-Pamlico watershed in Eastern North Carolina, and studied non-monetary benefits of education through a social capital lens (if you are interested in learning more about me and my work, visit <a href="https://sites.google.com/gmx.net/kyrahagge/home"> my website <a/>).

At the COAST Lab, I am working on the nascent <a href="https://www.uncw.edu/news/2025/12/uncw-receives-grant-to-advance-flooding-research"> Coastal Community Flood Resilience Initiative <a/> located at the <a href="https://uncw.edu/cms/">Center for Marine Science<a/>. By bringing organizations together to identify common resources and gaps, we university researchers can assist the communities living in New Hanover County in dealing with flooding in a tangible way.  <br>
When I am not behind a computer researching or talking to interest-holders, you can find me volunteering for various social and environmental organizations, playing with my kid at one of the many playgrounds in and around Wilmington, or catching waves or dinner between Wrightsville Beach and Bald Head Island. Originally from Switzerland, I also travel to Europe frequently and enjoy hiking, mountain-biking and immersing myself in other cultures/languages.
